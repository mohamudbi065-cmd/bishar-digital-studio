Ebola Virus Disease: Public Health Awareness, Prevention and Safety Guidance

Published: 25 November 2026 | Category: Public Health Information | Location: Kenya
PUBLIC HEALTH AWARENESS NOTICE

The Kenya Red Cross shared public health information attributed to the Ministry of Health concerning Ebola Virus Disease. This article presents the awareness information for community education.

1. What Is Ebola Virus Disease?

Ebola Virus Disease (EVD) is a rare but serious infectious disease caused by the Ebola virus. It can be severe and sometimes fatal.

Public awareness, early reporting and appropriate medical care are important in preventing further transmission.

2. How Does Ebola Spread?

A. Animal-to-Human Transmission

Ebola can spread from infected animals to humans through contact with infected animals or their body fluids.

B. Human-to-Human Transmission

Transmission can occur through direct contact with the body fluids of an infected person, including blood, sweat, urine, vomit and other body fluids.

C. Contaminated Surfaces

Infection can also occur through contact with objects and surfaces contaminated with infected body fluids.

3. Who Is at Risk?

People may be at risk following exposure to the virus. Groups with increased exposure risks include:

  • People who have close contact with an infected person.
  • Healthcare workers caring for suspected or confirmed patients.
  • Hunters and people handling potentially infected wildlife.
  • People handling contaminated materials.
  • Travellers to and from affected regions.

4. Signs and Symptoms of Ebola

Possible symptoms include:

  • Fever.
  • Headache.
  • General body weakness.
  • Muscle pain.
  • Sore throat.
  • Vomiting.
  • Diarrhoea.
  • Skin rash.
  • Bleeding in some cases.

Symptoms may appear between 2 and 21 days after exposure.

Important:

These symptoms can also occur with other illnesses. Having a fever or headache does not automatically mean someone has Ebola. Anyone with possible symptoms following relevant exposure should contact health authorities promptly.

5. Prevention of Ebola Disease

Important preventive measures include:

  1. Avoid direct contact with body fluids from suspected or confirmed Ebola patients.
  2. Wash hands regularly using soap and running water.
  3. Use hand sanitiser containing at least 70% alcohol where appropriate.
  4. Avoid handling potentially contaminated clothing, bedding and materials.
  5. Healthcare workers should use appropriate protective equipment.
  6. Ensure safe and dignified burials through trained burial teams.
  7. Follow official public health guidance.

6. Treatment and Medical Care

Anyone suspected of having Ebola requires prompt assessment by qualified healthcare professionals. Supportive medical care is important in managing the disease.

Do not attempt to treat a suspected Ebola infection at home or handle potentially contaminated materials yourself.

7. Ebola Testing and Preparedness in Kenya

The Ministry of Health has identified designated laboratory facilities and arrangements for testing, isolation and management of suspected Ebola cases.

Testing and patient management should be conducted through authorized health facilities and qualified healthcare professionals.

8. Advice to Travellers

  • Avoid unnecessary travel to areas currently reporting Ebola outbreaks.
  • Follow official travel and public health advisories.
  • If you have travelled to an affected area and develop possible symptoms within 21 days, contact health authorities immediately.
  • Inform healthcare professionals about your travel history and possible exposure.

9. Isolation and Reporting of Suspected Cases

Anyone who has had a relevant exposure and develops possible Ebola symptoms should:

  • Limit contact with other people while seeking professional guidance.
  • Contact health authorities for instructions.
  • Report possible exposure and symptoms promptly.
  • Follow instructions from qualified healthcare professionals.

10. Safe and Dignified Burials

Safe and dignified burial practices are important in preventing transmission. Families and communities should seek assistance from trained burial teams and health authorities.

11. Kenya's Ebola Situation: Important Context

The Ministry of Health previously confirmed an imported Ebola case in Kenya. Public health authorities have continued surveillance and preparedness activities.

The awareness message shared by the Kenya Red Cross on 23 November 2026 should not, by itself, be interpreted as confirmation of a newly detected case on that date.

Readers are encouraged to consult the latest official Ministry of Health announcements for the current outbreak status.
